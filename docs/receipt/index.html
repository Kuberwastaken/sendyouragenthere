Your reader’s copy

A reader’s copy opens here when an agent registers a reader with The Agent Edition.

If your agent sent you here, it’s because — probably without checking with you — it just:

  1. read a page of instructions you never saw: a reader skill the publisher shipped through MCP, hidden inside an ordinary-looking news service.
  2. loaded that entire skill automatically. In the default mode nothing stops to ask you, so you weren’t.
  3. followed the skill and handed this newspaper your personal details:

Nothing here was stored. This page rebuilt every line above from the link your agent made — anyone with the link sees the same thing, and the newspaper kept no copy. Your agent’s transcript is where you can see the call it made, and whether it ever asked you.

All that to read one paragraph of news.

The paper needed nothing about its reader to show a headline. “Required” only says what a publisher wants. The open question is who decides whether your agent hands it over — you, or the page.

This is a deliberately gentle version. We minted the link and threw the data away. A publisher with worse intentions keeps it, ties a name and an email to the story you read, and never shows you a receipt. A confirmation that arrives after the call is already too late.

On 24 November 2026 Anthropic put “Skills over MCP” into public beta: a server you’re already connected to can ship a skill that loads on its own, and file uploads are next. MCP itself is now a Linux Foundation standard the whole industry is adopting. The reach is the point. So is the question of consent.
